What am I looking at?

This is the fold, a reading and research surface, demoing on one night: the sinking of the Titanic, April 1912.

The workspace holds five readings and three recordings — a Senate speech, the British inquiry's report and its own numbers table, a survivor's memoir, a newspaper front page read by OCR, film footage and a 1912 tribute record.

Nothing is taken on memory: every sentence the surface shows is traced back to where it appears.

What to look at

Open a source

Click any source — the Senate speech, Beesley's memoir, the Tribune front page. Its names, figures and dates are marked where they appear, and a passage carries its origin back to the text it was read from.

Where the accounts disagree on the death toll

The Board of Trade report says "the loss of 1,490 lives"; Lawrence Beesley's memoir says "815 of her passengers and 688 of her crew were drowned" — 1,503. The report's own "Numbers saved" table lets you check its arithmetic: 2,201 aboard, 711 saved. The surface shows the disagreement rather than picking a winner.

The film, with its read title cards

The 1912 footage plays here, and its captions are a machine reading of the film's own title cards, with timecodes. The second film has no captions — transcribe it to see what the machine hears.

Machine-read sources are flagged

The Senate speech (scan-derived text), the Tribune front page (OCR from the pixels) and the film's captions are machine readings, and each source's note says so. Quote the source, not the machine's reading of it.

Known limits

  • Synthesis no source states is not produced: if the sources do not say it, the surface says it is missing.
  • Machine readings slip. The known misses: OCR errors outside the Fort's confusion table, generic headings made of ordinary words, and run-together names where one side is a single word.
  • The demo corpus is small on purpose — five readings and three recordings, excerpted to what the demo needs. The full sources are at the addresses each one cites.
  • Conversational answers (not in this preview) run a small model in your browser (or a local Ollama). The record itself — the sources, search and the record's own best answer — works without one.
