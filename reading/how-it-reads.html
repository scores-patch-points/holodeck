How it reads — the priors behind every word

No model is called. Each written form is looked up in received tables: a part-of-speech prior from a human-annotated treebank, and a morphology prior from UniMorph. Every answer names the store that gave it, every hop taken to reach it, and every miss stays a typed gap.

Where the priors come from

The class prior counts every tag a form received across a treebank of 12,544 hand-annotated sentences, and keeps ambiguity rather than picking a winner. The morphology prior keeps only UniMorph's irregular tail; regular inflection is recovered by rule at read time. Priors are derived and checked against the Zenodotus corpus (24 categories, 2,100+ source documents); its POS-vocabulary gate was validated on 10 deliberately diverse specimens.
